## PROJECT OBJECTIVE & OUTCOME

**Objective:** Profound is a Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) tool, effectively the equivalent of SEO but for generative AI platforms, optimizing how Cigna's web content is surfaced and represented by LLM-based systems. The objective was to take this vendor solution from approved-for-evaluation through to a compliant production deployment on Cigna's websites. By the time I picked it up, the solution had already cleared initial legal, architecture, and information security approval. My job was to lead it through the full enterprise readiness process and get it safely turned on in production.

**Outcome:** Delivered a compliant production deployment of the Profound GEO solution. Key results:

- Brought the vendor solution through enterprise architecture and guardrail review to production go-live
- Put the correct contracting addendums in place to cover the vendor relationship and data handling
- Defined the human approval roles and responsibilities for changes to key infrastructure, so production changes had clear ownership and oversight
- Supported testing to confirm the solution behaved as expected before go-live
- Inventoried risks and confirmed stakeholder satisfaction before turning the solution on in production

## LESSONS LEARNED

1. When you inherit a project that has already cleared initial approvals, the real work is everything between approval and production. The approval is permission to start, not evidence that the hard part is done.
2. With vendor solutions, half the job is eliciting information out of someone else's system. Enterprise review needs answers the vendor holds, and getting those answers in a usable form was as much of the work as anything internal.
3. Defining who approves changes to key infrastructure is not paperwork, it is risk management. Naming that human ownership before go-live is what keeps a launched solution from drifting out of compliance later.

## PMI APPLICATION NARRATIVE

Profound is a Generative Engine Optimization tool, which is essentially SEO but aimed at generative AI platforms instead of search engines. It optimizes how our web content gets surfaced and represented by LLM-based systems. We were rolling it out across Cigna's websites, and I led the project that took it from approved to live in production.

By the time the project reached me, it had already passed initial legal, architecture, and information security approval. That approval was the starting line. My job was to lead the team through the full enterprise readiness process and get the solution safely turned on. I kicked off with the team, charted a roadmap of the steps we needed to take to comply with enterprise standards, and scheduled the demo sessions with key approvers and stakeholders that would carry us through architecture and guardrail review.

A lot of this work was vendor-facing. Profound's solution sat on their systems, so a recurring part of the job was eliciting the information enterprise review needed out of the vendor and getting it into a form our reviewers could act on. Alongside that, I worked with legal to put the correct contracting addendums in place, and I defined the roles and responsibilities for human approval of changes to key infrastructure, so that once the solution was live, changes to anything sensitive had clear ownership and oversight rather than happening informally.

I also supported testing to confirm the solution did what it was supposed to before we exposed it in production. When work slowed or deliverable timelines started to slip, I re-engaged the team to keep us moving against the roadmap. We passed our architecture and guardrail reviews, I inventoried the risks, and I confirmed every stakeholder was satisfied before we turned the solution on. The project closed with Profound running in production under a defined human approval model for ongoing infrastructure changes.
