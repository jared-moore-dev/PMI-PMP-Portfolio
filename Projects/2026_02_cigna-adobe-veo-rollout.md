## PROJECT OBJECTIVE & OUTCOME

**Objective:** Adobe Veo is a generative AI package that sits on top of the existing Adobe product suite. It can use either your own models or Adobe's custom models to generate images and touch up features for ad copy. The objective was to take this vendor solution from approved-for-evaluation through to a compliant production deployment, following the same enterprise readiness process used for other AI solutions, so that marketing and creative teams could use the capability within enterprise guardrails.

**Outcome:** Delivered a compliant production deployment of Adobe Veo. Key results:

- Brought the vendor solution through enterprise architecture and guardrail review to production go-live
- Managed a mid-project change in the chief architect role, adapting to a shifted stakeholder set without losing review momentum
- Put the correct contracting and data-handling terms in place for the vendor relationship
- Defined human approval roles and responsibilities for changes to key infrastructure
- Inventoried risks and confirmed stakeholder satisfaction before turning the solution on in production

## LESSONS LEARNED

1. A key stakeholder leaving mid-project is a risk you manage, not an excuse you hold onto. When the chief architect changed roles, the choice was to either treat it as a reset or to bring the incoming stakeholders up to speed and protect the progress already made. Doing the latter is what kept the project on track.
2. Continuity of review is its own deliverable. The reviews we had already passed only held value if the new stakeholders trusted them, so part of the transition work was making the prior progress legible to people who had not been in the room for it.
3. As with any vendor solution, eliciting the right information out of the vendor's systems was a large share of the work, and starting that early left room to absorb the surprises that came later.

## PMI APPLICATION NARRATIVE

Adobe Veo is a generative AI package that sits on top of the existing Adobe product suite. It can generate images and touch up features for ad copy, using either our own models or Adobe's custom models. We were bringing it into Cigna for our marketing and creative work, and I led the project that took it from approved to live in production.

Like the other vendor solutions I handled, by the time the project reached me it had already passed initial legal, architecture, and information security approval. I kicked off with the team, charted a roadmap of the steps we needed to comply with enterprise standards, and scheduled the demo sessions with approvers and stakeholders that would move us through architecture and guardrail review. A good portion of the work was vendor-facing, since the capability lived on Adobe's side, so I spent real effort eliciting the information enterprise review needed out of their systems and getting it into a form our reviewers could act on. In parallel I worked with legal on the contracting and data-handling terms, and I defined the human approval roles and responsibilities for changes to key infrastructure.

The defining challenge on this project was a stakeholder change. Partway through, our chief architect changed roles. That shifted the stakeholder set I was working with, and it meant some of the people who had context on our reviews were no longer the ones making decisions. I had a choice between treating that as a reset or protecting the progress we had already made. I chose the latter. I brought the incoming stakeholders up to speed, made the reviews we had already passed legible to people who had not been in the room for them, and kept us moving through the remaining gates rather than restarting.

When work slowed or timelines slipped, I re-engaged the team to keep us on the roadmap. We passed our architecture and guardrail reviews, I inventoried the risks, and I confirmed every stakeholder was satisfied before turning the solution on. The project closed with Adobe Veo running in production under a defined human approval model for ongoing infrastructure changes.
