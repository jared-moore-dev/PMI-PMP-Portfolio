## PROJECT OBJECTIVE & OUTCOME

**Objective:**
Cigna Ventures was evaluating AI vendors for potential investment and partnership, and they needed someone who could actually test the products rather than just sit through demos. My role was to stand each vendor solution up in Cigna's environment, run it through a structured assessment covering technical capability, security, data privacy, values alignment, and portfolio fit, and deliver a clear recommendation the Ventures team could act on.

**Outcome:**
Delivered technical due diligence assessments and formal go/no-go recommendations across multiple vendor engagements over 7 months. Several vendors that looked credible in demos did not hold up under independent testing. Security and data privacy gaps were identified and documented in recommendation packages, giving Ventures partners the specifics they needed to either negotiate remediation or walk away. Every recommendation was grounded in empirical testing in Cigna's own environment, not vendor-controlled conditions.

## YOUR ROLE & RESPONSIBILITIES

**Role:** Technical Due Diligence Lead

## PMI PROCESS GROUP COVERAGE

### Initiating (IN)
- Scoped each vendor evaluation with Cigna Ventures partners; defined evaluation objectives, assessment dimensions, and success criteria at the outset of each engagement
- Identified stakeholders: Ventures partners, vendor contacts, internal security and compliance reviewers, and business sponsors considering the vendor relationship
- Established access and environment requirements for standing up each vendor product within Cigna's infrastructure

### Planning (PL)
- Developed technical test plans for each vendor evaluation: specific capabilities to test, test scenarios, and evidence standards needed to support a credible recommendation
- Planned evaluation sequencing: technical integration first to validate baseline capability, then security and privacy review, values and ethics assessment, and portfolio fit analysis
- Coordinated with Ventures partners on evaluation timelines, vendor engagement protocols, and recommendation format expectations

### Executing (EX)
- Stood up vendor AI products within Cigna's internal environment; configured integrations, ran test scenarios, and documented observed behavior against vendor claims
- Conducted security and data privacy reviews: data flows, storage practices, access controls, compliance documentation
- Ran values and ethics assessments through behavioral testing and edge case probing; challenged vendor claims with empirical evidence where representations did not match what the product actually did
- Shared interim findings with Ventures partners throughout each engagement; aligned on emerging conclusions before finalizing reports
- Drafted and delivered formal recommendation reports with a clear go/no-go/conditional call and supporting evidence

### Monitoring & Controlling (MC)
- Tracked evaluation progress against scope and timeline per engagement; managed concurrent evaluations across the 7-month period
- Held consistent evidentiary standards across all recommendation outputs; every report had to support a confident investment or partnership decision
- Escalated material security or values findings to Ventures partners immediately when identified, not at the end of the engagement

### Closing (CL)
- Delivered final recommendation reports to Ventures partners as the formal close of each vendor engagement
- Archived evaluation documentation — test results, security findings, assessment records — for future reference or re-engagement
- Transitioned out of vendor vetting responsibilities in July 2024 as focus shifted to the MDLive technical project lead role

## RISKS & ISSUES

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vendor capability misrepresentation | High | High | Mandatory live testing in Cigna's environment for every vendor; no recommendation based on vendor-supplied demos alone |
| Security or data privacy gaps in vendor products | Medium | Critical | Structured security and privacy review as a required gate; material findings escalated immediately and documented in recommendation |
| Values or ethics misalignment not visible in demos | Medium | High | Behavioral testing under edge case and adversarial scenarios; values alignment assessed against observable output, not vendor claims |
| Portfolio overlap creating redundant investment | Medium | Medium | Explicit portfolio fit analysis required for every evaluation; findings included in all recommendation reports |
| Vendor engagement delays impacting evaluation timeline | Medium | Low | Maintained concurrent evaluation pipeline; sequenced engagements to avoid single-vendor dependency on throughput |

**Issues Resolved:**
- Identified multiple instances where vendor-claimed capabilities did not reproduce under independent testing in Cigna's environment; findings documented and surfaced in recommendation reports
- Detected security and data privacy practices that did not meet Cigna's internal standards; documented with enough specificity for Ventures partners to negotiate remediation or decline the relationship

## LESSONS LEARNED

1. Vendor demos are a starting point, not evidence. Several vendors looked strong in controlled demos and fell apart when we stood their product up in our own environment. Building that step into the process as a non-negotiable changed the quality of every recommendation we made.
2. Values alignment has to be tested behaviorally, not taken on faith. Vendor policy statements about fairness and ethics are easy to produce. What the product actually does under edge cases is what matters. That is what we tested.
3. Procurement risk shows up at the beginning or not at all. Security gaps and portfolio conflicts that surfaced mid-evaluation were almost always traceable to assumptions nobody checked at the start. Defining evaluation criteria before touching a vendor product consistently produced better findings than going in open-ended.

## PMI APPLICATION NARRATIVE

As Technical Due Diligence Lead for Cigna Ventures from January through July 2024, I led the technical evaluation of AI vendor products under consideration for investment, partnership, or operational adoption within Cigna's portfolio. Cigna Ventures needed an independent technical perspective on whether these products actually did what vendors claimed, whether they were safe to bring into Cigna's environment, and whether they fit within the company's existing technology portfolio.

My process for each vendor started the same way: scoping the evaluation with the Ventures team before any vendor contact, defining the capabilities we were testing, the security and privacy standards we were applying, and the portfolio fit criteria relevant to the use case. I then stood each product up inside Cigna's internal environment and ran it through a structured test plan. This was deliberate. Vendor demo environments are controlled in ways that production environments are not. Testing in our own infrastructure exposed real behavior, and in several cases that behavior did not match what had been represented.

After technical testing I ran security and data privacy reviews, looking at data handling practices, access controls, and compliance documentation. I also ran values and ethics assessments, which I treated as empirical work rather than a policy review. That meant testing AI output under edge cases and adversarial inputs to look for bias and fairness gaps, not accepting vendor statements about their training practices. Material findings from either review went to the Ventures team immediately rather than waiting for the final report.

Each engagement closed with a formal recommendation report covering all four dimensions: technical capability, security and privacy, values and ethics, and portfolio fit. The Ventures team used those reports to make investment and partnership decisions. The ones where I flagged significant gaps gave them the documentation they needed to negotiate remediation requirements or decline the relationship with a clear record of why.
