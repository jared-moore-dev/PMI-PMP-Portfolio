## PROJECT OBJECTIVE & OUTCOME

**Objective:**
Part of Cigna's Summer Innovation Program. The challenge was finding a meaningful way to improve health outcomes for insured members. Our team landed on eating habits as the target and built toward connecting healthy behavior to something members actually cared about: lower premiums.

**Outcome:**
We shipped a working full-stack web application within the 3-month window. My Cigna Chef let users upload and share recipes, set personal health goals, earn points for hitting them, and apply those points toward premium reductions. It integrated Okta for authentication, a payment processor for the rewards engine, and a custom REST API layer. We published the codebase to GitHub and presented to a panel of Cigna executives at the Summer Innovation Showcase. We did not place, but we delivered a complete, integrated product with a team of 11 interns in 11 weeks.

- Ran a team skills inventory to assess what we had and structured the team accordingly
- Identified stakeholders: project sponsor, intern mentor, executive judge panel, and Cigna members as the end-user population

### Monitoring & Controlling (MC)
- Tracked sprint progress against backlog commitments through the Kanban board and burndown
- Ran retrospectives after each sprint and actually used them to change how we worked the following sprint
- Caught an early leadership problem where over-collaboration was slowing decisions and costing us time; course-corrected by owning prioritization and scope calls more clearly
- Held scope boundaries so the team stayed focused on what mattered within the 11-week window

## LESSONS LEARNED

1. Ambiguity in success criteria is a scope risk. If you can't define what done looks like, you can't plan toward it. We resolved the "what is healthy" problem during planning by letting users define their own goals — that decision unblocked everything downstream.
2. Collaborative leadership and decisive leadership are not the same thing. I learned early that being inclusive about input and being clear about decisions are separate acts. Conflating them slowed us down. The fix was knowing when I was gathering opinions and when I was making a call.
3. Agile ceremonies create accountability where informal teams have none. For 11 interns with no shared history, standups, planning sessions, and retros gave us a common language for where we were and what was blocking us.

## PMI APPLICATION NARRATIVE

As Project Manager for Cigna's 2022 Summer Innovation Program, I led a cross-functional team of 11 interns to design and deliver My Cigna Chef — a full-stack wellness application aimed at encouraging healthy eating habits among Cigna's insured population and connecting those behaviors to premium-reduction rewards.

I was responsible for the full project management lifecycle across an 11-week sprint cycle. During initiation, I facilitated team ideation sessions alongside our project sponsor and mentor, aligned on the solution concept, and conducted a skills inventory that I used to organize the team into frontend and backend workstreams. A central planning challenge was defining what "healthy eating" actually meant for a diverse user base. I resolved this by designing user-defined health goal tracks with pre-approved reward pathways — a scope decision that gave the team something measurable to build toward and unblocked product design.

I structured execution across three Agile sprints. Sprint 1 covered solution ideation, Figma prototyping, and stakeholder alignment. Sprint 2 delivered UI/UX iteration, backend data modeling, and integration scaffolding for Okta and our payment processor. Sprint 3 focused on full-stack integration, end-to-end testing, GitHub publication, and presentation preparation. I facilitated daily standups, sprint planning, reviews, and retrospectives throughout.

A key leadership adjustment happened mid-project. I recognized through retrospective reflection that my over-collaborative approach to decisions was creating bottlenecks and slowing sprint velocity. I course-corrected by taking clearer ownership of scope and prioritization decisions while keeping the team in the loop on execution. The final sprint moved significantly faster as a result.

The project closed with a formal presentation to a panel of Cigna executives and judges, and the full codebase was published to GitHub. We did not place in the competition, but we delivered a functional, integrated software product — authentication, rewards logic, a scalable API — within a fixed 11-week window.
