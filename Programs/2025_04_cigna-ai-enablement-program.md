## PROJECT OBJECTIVE & OUTCOME

**Objective:**
Every AI solution that ships at Cigna has to pass through a governance pipeline before it goes to production. The program exists to make sure that happens — and that when it does, the evidence trail is complete enough to hold up under a regulatory audit or a production incident investigation. My role in the program is to run that pipeline for my portion of the portfolio, taking product teams from intake through architecture review, vendor safety assessment, testing validation, and final production sign-off.

**Outcome:**
Over the credited period I personally managed approximately 130 AI solution projects through the full pipeline as part of a 4-person team covering 400+ enterprise AI deployments total. Every approved project has a documented evidence trail covering testing, safety, fairness, and vendor risk. The governance framework was also adapted during this period to cover agentic AI architectures, which the original model was not built to handle.

## LESSONS LEARNED

1. At program scale, consistency matters more than perfection on any single project. Managing 130+ concurrent projects means small inconsistencies in how evidence is reviewed or documented compound into real audit exposure. A consistent review standard applied across every project, regardless of team size or complexity, is the highest-leverage thing you can do.
2. Governance frameworks have to keep up with the technology they govern. The framework I inherited was built for traditional and predictive AI. When agentic architectures started showing up in the portfolio, it had gaps. Catching that early and pushing for updated guidance was the right call — forcing new use cases into criteria that did not fit them would have created risk that nobody would have seen until something went wrong.
3. What you set up at kickoff determines how painful approval is. The most common source of delay was product teams discovering evidence requirements late in their development cycle. Getting requirements on paper at intake and making sure teams understood them upfront cut down on late-stage remediation significantly.

## PMI APPLICATION NARRATIVE

As AI Enablement Engineer at Cigna, I manage the enterprise AI production readiness pipeline for my portion of the organization's AI portfolio. The program's purpose is straightforward: before any AI solution goes to production, it has to be tested, reviewed, and documented to a standard that would hold up under a regulatory audit or a post-incident investigation. My job is to make sure that happens for every project I own and that the evidence trail behind each approval is complete.

Over the credited period I managed approximately 130 AI solution projects through the full pipeline as part of a four-person team covering 400 or more enterprise AI deployments. For each project, my process starts at intake with a kickoff call where I establish the governance scope, identify any third-party AI dependencies, and set clear expectations for what evidence needs to be produced before I will sign off. Getting that conversation right at the start matters. The most common source of delay in the pipeline is product teams discovering requirements late in their development cycle, and a thorough kickoff call is the most effective way to prevent it.

After kickoff, I run an architecture review to assess technical design, data flows, and AI component integration against Cigna's governance standards. For any project with a third-party AI dependency, I coordinate the Vendor Safety Questionnaire process, review the responses, and flag risk gaps before the project advances. Once a team submits their testing evidence package, I review it across five dimensions: end-to-end testing, safety and fairness, bias evaluation, adversarial testing, and load testing. I hold formal production approval authority for my portfolio. Nothing in it goes to production without my sign-off.

One of the more significant challenges that came up during this period was agentic AI. As teams started submitting agentic solutions for review, it became clear the inherited governance framework had not been designed with those architectures in mind. The risk patterns are different, and applying the existing criteria would have meant approving solutions under standards that did not actually reflect what they were doing. I worked with my team, architecture, and legal to identify the gaps and develop updated guidance, which became the basis for how we now handle agentic submissions.

The work is ongoing. The pipeline has processed over 400 enterprise AI projects across the team, each with a documented audit trail behind it.
